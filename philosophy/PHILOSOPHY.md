# Intention
## Narrative-Driven Building

> *The story of how you got there matters more than the specs of what you built.*

---

## Origin

This started as a footer line — "Built with intention." — first on a personal website, then a CV, then CV Studio. Each time it felt right, but undefined.

Now it's becoming clearer: **intention is the connective tissue between past, philosophy, and product.**

---

## The Problem with "90% Better"

Traditional resumes and product marketing lead with metrics:
- "Increased efficiency by 90%"
- "Reduced costs by 40%"
- "Scaled to 1M users"

These are *outcomes*, not *stories*. They're forgettable because they lack context:
- What problem did you actually see?
- Why did you choose this solution?
- What did you sacrifice to get there?
- How does your past explain your present?

---

## What Narrative-Driven Building Means

**For Products:**
- Origin story → Design decisions → Success criteria
- Not "what does it do?" but "why does it exist?"

**For Careers (CVs):**
- Journey → Role context → Philosophy → Impact
- Not "what did you build?" but "what did you learn that changed how you build?"

**For Companies:**
- Founder's past → Market insight → Product philosophy → Culture
- Not "what's the feature set?" but "what truth did you see that others missed?"

---

## The Through-Line

```
Your Past → Your Philosophy → Your Product → Your Impact
    ↑                                              ↓
    └────────────── Success Criteria ←─────────────┘
```

Success isn't just hitting metrics. It's whether the product fulfilled its *intention*.
